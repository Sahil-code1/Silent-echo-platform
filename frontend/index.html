About & privacy

What SilentEcho protects, and what it cannot promise

Plain-language answers about how pseudonymous reporting works, what is stored, and where the limits are.

What SilentEcho is

SilentEcho is a reporting and case-management platform. You can report bullying, harassment, discrimination or another sensitive concern to an organisation without giving your name, email address or phone number.

Responders in that organisation can ask questions, request evidence and update you, through a single case that you can follow from your dashboard.

How pseudonymous reporting works

You are identified to SilentEcho by a private sign-in ID. Each case has its own Case ID, which refers to the case but does not let anyone sign in.

Organisation members see the case, its status and your messages. They do not see your sign-in ID or your internal account identifier.

How your private sign-in ID works

The server generates the ID with 256 bits of randomness and shows it once. The database stores only a keyed HMAC-SHA-256 digest, never the ID itself.

Save it when you are shown it. If you lose it, it cannot be recovered and you lose access to your account and cases. Anyone who has the ID can sign in, so treat it like a password.

How complaint information and evidence are handled

Complaint titles and descriptions, messages, timeline notes, evidence filenames and evidence files are encrypted on the server with AES-256-GCM before they are stored.

Evidence must be a PDF, JPG, PNG or TXT file of up to 2 MB. The server checks the file contents, not only the name. Files are stored outside the public web directory under random names. Closed cases do not accept new evidence.

Privacy protections in place

  • Sign-in IDs are stored only as keyed digests.
  • Content is encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM.
  • Cases are visible only to the reporter and the owning organisation.
  • Sessions expire after 30 minutes of inactivity and end at sign-out.
  • Repeated failed sign-ins from one address are slowed down.
  • The audit trail records actions, not report text.

What SilentEcho cannot promise

  • It is not end-to-end encrypted. The operator controls the encryption key.
  • It is not anonymous. Your writing, dates, places and files can identify you.
  • File metadata, such as author names or camera data, is not removed from uploads.
  • Uploads are not scanned for malware.
  • A lost sign-in ID cannot be recovered.
  • Organisations are not independently verified.
  • There is no retention or deletion schedule yet.
  • It is not an emergency service and cases are not watched in real time.

Why pseudonymous is not necessarily anonymous

A pseudonym hides your name, not your story. The organisation still knows what you have reported, and the details of your report may point to you: your writing style, dates, places, the people you mention, and any file you upload.

Think about what you include before you submit. You can leave out details that are not needed to understand the concern.

Why SilentEcho is not end-to-end encrypted

With end-to-end encryption, only the people in the conversation hold the keys, so the service itself cannot read the content. SilentEcho does not work that way.

The server encrypts content with a key that the operator controls. Encryption at rest protects stored data if the database or disk is copied without the key. It does not protect against the operator or against a compromised application.

Support beyond this platform

SilentEcho can receive and handle a report. It cannot respond to an emergency, and it is not a substitute for people who can help you in person.

If you are in immediate danger

Contact your local emergency number now. Do not wait for a reply to a message on SilentEcho.

  • Move somewhere you feel safer, if you can do so.
  • Tell someone nearby what is happening.

Talk to someone you trust

A friend, family member, teacher, manager or doctor can help you think through your options before, during or after a report.

  • You do not have to decide everything on your own.
  • They can help you prepare for a conversation with the organisation.

Your institution or workplace

Counselling, student welfare, human resources or a designated safeguarding contact may be able to support you and explain the process.

Ask what happens after a report, who can see it, and how long it takes.

After you submit a report

  1. Save your Case ID somewhere private.
  2. Check My Cases and your messages regularly.
  3. Keep your own private notes: dates, places and what happened.
  4. Tell the team if your situation changes or you feel unsafe.
  5. Take breaks. Reporting can be stressful.
SilentEcho is not an emergency-response service. It is not monitored in real time, cannot send help, and does not replace the police, medical services or emergency services. How an organisation responds depends on its own policies and staffing.
